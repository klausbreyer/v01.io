# Headline

A good title makes a claim, shows a person, or names a problem. A label does none of these. "How We Work Now" is a label. "Alone With Agents, I Am Faster Than My Team" is a title.

## How to work

1. Read the whole post. Do not write a title from the frontmatter or the first paragraph alone.
2. Answer one question first: **which problem does this post solve for the reader?** If the post only shares something, say so. The title cannot fix that.
3. Look for the sharpest sentence that is already in the post. Often it is the title.
4. Write **ten variants** that use at least five different patterns from the list below.
5. Name one favorite and one safe choice, each with a single sentence of reasoning.
6. Change the `title` in the frontmatter only after Klaus picks one.

## Patterns

Each pattern comes with titles Klaus collected as examples.

**First person, with a confession or a decision.** The author is in the title and has something at stake.
- I'm going back to writing code by hand
- I returned to AWS - and was reminded HARD why I left.
- Apple is increasing my cortisol levels
- The Time I Lied to the CTO and Saved the Day
- why I can't stop thinking about Papua New Guinea and what I think everyone should know about it

**A command.** Tell the reader what to do or what to stop.
- Don't be a meat proxy
- Stop calling yourself an IC
- Please, Stop the Absurd Coding Challenges
- just fucking use html
- Let me fix your 1:1 meetings in 90 seconds.

**Declare that a thing is not a thing.** It never existed, or it no longer matters.
- There is no such thing as a UX strategy
- DevOps Isn't Dead, but It's Not in Great Health Either
- Lean UX: Getting out of the delivery business

**A contradiction.** Two halves that should not fit together.
- A shell colon does nothing. Use it anyway
- Nothing Works and Everyone Is Euphoric
- Simplicity is an advantage but sadly complexity sells better
- Osmo Wiio: Communication usually fails, except by accident

**A turn at the end.** The last words change how the first words read.
- Swift sucks at web serving... or does it?
- Shopify acquires Threads (no, not that one)
- Group Chat: The Best Way to Totally Stress Out Your Team
- Modern WordPress - Yikes!

**A concrete number.** The number carries the effort or the scale.
- 50 Hours to Draw Some Lines
- What do I think about Lua after shipping a project with 60,000 lines of code?

**A known phrase, bent.** The reader hears the original and notices the change.
- Houston, we have a solution
- Sir, there's a cat in your mirror dimension
- An Engineer and an LLM Log into a Tech Interview: Are They Cheating?
- In Loving Memory of Square Checkbox
- The unreasonable effectiveness of HTML

**A strong claim or an open question.** State it flat, without a hedge.
- A Fundamental Principle of Aeronautical Engineering Has Been Overturned
- Your brain was never designed for this much bad news
- Twitter is now attention roulette and ultimately meaningless
- Is artificial consciousness achievable? Lessons from the human brain
- Systems Ideas that Sound Good But Almost Never Work: "Let's just..."

**A plain, useful promise.** No trick. The title says exactly what the reader gets.
- How to do a successful Hacker News launch
- A mostly complete guide to React rendering behavior
- What UI density means and how to design for it
- Some more things about Django I've been enjoying
- I made a free Figma library packed with components for fast prototyping

## Rules

- The title must be true. If the post does not back the claim, change the title or tell Klaus the post is missing something.
- Prefer concrete words, people and numbers over abstract nouns.
- Follow the title rules in `AGENTS.md`: quoted, title case for English, no em or en dashes.
- Launch posts in the `Shipped` category keep their own convention: the name, then what it is (see `2026/08/texttile`).
- German posts get German titles. Do not translate an English pun that does not work in German.
- A new title does not change the folder name or the URL of a published post.
